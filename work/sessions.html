The Problem

Focus apps are built for one person

Students go to the library at night because working near other people makes it easier to start. Study apps give them a timer, a streak counter and a leaderboard of strangers.

How students already study

I asked classmates where they study, who with, and what makes them show up.

Place is already the strategy

They pick a library or café for the people in it, not the desk.

Streaks need an audience

Everyone had abandoned a streak. Nobody remembered anyone noticing.

Joining is easier than planning

Planning a session over text kills it. Walking into one already running does not.

The Reframe

From a private timer to a visible one

Asking how to help someone focus longer only produced solo features. The better question was what changes when friends can see the timer.

Before

"Focus for 25 minutes."

A countdown with nothing behind it

After

"7 friends studying now."

A session other people can see and join

Principles

Rules I set before drawing screens

A social layer can turn competitive, noisy or too revealing. Three rules kept it from that.

The app never asks you to start

Friends are visible on the map, but nothing nudges you to begin.

Celebrate less than feels natural

Streaks and challenges interrupt only when something real happened.

Public or private, decided up front

Chosen before the timer starts, never a default to undo later.

Process

Mapping every path through the app

Laying the whole product out as one flow exposed the biggest gap: what a session looks like to everyone not in it.

System Design

A visual language borrowed from iOS

Helvetica Neue, soft blues and glass surfaces keep it familiar, and quiet enough to leave open beside your notes for hours.

Final Walkthrough

Onboarding

Setup asks what you study, when, and in what kind of room. The answers shape session suggestions later.

Starting a session

Name it, pick public or private, set a length, and go. Once it runs, the screen gets out of the way.

Seeing who is already studying

Friends appear as pins with the time left on their timer. A group session is one tap to join.

Adding friends

Search, invite directly, or accept a suggestion from mutual friends. No feed and no follower count.

Challenges and history

Weekly challenges give a group a shared goal. History is a plain record of the time put in.

When a streak breaks

The screen states what happened and holds the number. It neither scolds nor hides the loss.

Reflections

Presence did more than any feature I built around it. Seeing others study motivated more reliably than counters or notifications.

Most of the hard decisions were about restraint: what to leave off the timer, how often to celebrate, and how to handle a broken streak.

Next I would design the map's quieter states: hotspots through the day, quiet hours, friends about to finish.
