Sessions

A social study accountability app that helps students stay locked in together. Sessions lets friends see each other studying in real time on a campus map, join public or private study sessions, and build streaks that keep momentum going.

The Problem

Studying alone is quiet. Staying accountable is loud.

College students often struggle to stay consistent. Existing study apps rely on solo timers, gamified streaks, or leaderboards that quickly lose meaning. What's missing is a way to feel the presence of others: the ambient motivation of knowing your friends are locked in too.

Isolation

Solo focus apps feel lonely and easy to abandon.

Empty gamification

Streaks and points without social meaning stop mattering.

No shared context

Study groups happen ad hoc. There's no place to see who's studying now.

The Reframe

From solo timer to social presence

I reframed the question from "How do we help students focus longer?" to "How do we make studying feel like a shared act?", turning the timer into a visible, joinable moment on a map.

Research

Why solo study apps don't stick

I looked at how peers actually study on campus and what makes them show up. Three patterns emerged that shaped the product.

01

Location is motivation

Students already choose libraries and cafés because being near others in the same mindset helps them focus.

02

Streaks need witnesses

A streak means nothing if no one knows. Public streaks with friends create real stakes.

03

Joining beats scheduling

Coordinating a study session is friction. Seeing one already happening removes it entirely.

Product Principles

Designing for calm, social focus

Presence over pressure

Show who's studying, but never guilt anyone into starting. Motivation should come from wanting to join in.

Craft over gamification

Streaks, milestones, and challenges are meaningful moments, not noisy notifications. Every celebration is earned.

Privacy by default

Every session is either public or private. Users control who sees them, where, and when.

Process

Mapping the end-to-end journey

I mapped every path a user could take: from onboarding, to starting a solo timer, to joining a group session on the map. This flow anchored every screen decision that followed.

System Design

A quiet, iOS-native visual language

Sessions uses Helvetica Neue with a restrained palette of soft blues, warm off-whites, and near-black text. The interface leans on glassmorphism, gentle gradients, and pill-shaped controls to feel native to iOS while keeping its own identity.

Final Walkthrough

Set a timer, get to work

Every session starts the same way: pick public or private, dial in a duration, and go. The screen quiets down as the timer starts. The focus is the work, not the app.

See who else is studying

The map is the heart of Sessions. Friends appear as pins with the time left in their session. Group sessions surface as joinable moments. Just tap and go.

Build a circle

Sessions is only as good as who you study with. Adding friends is intentional: search, invite, or accept suggestions based on mutual friends.

Mourn the streak

Losing a streak is feedback too, and it is the moment the tone matters most. It stays honest, never mocking, never over-eager. "You went cold" hits harder than a red X ever could.

Weekly challenges & session history

Group challenges give friends a shared goal for the week. Session history gives you a quiet, honest look at what you actually put in.

Onboarding & study preferences

Onboarding sets up who you are and how you study: subjects, times, style, and setting. These preferences quietly shape session suggestions later.

Reflections

Sessions taught me that ambient presence is a design material. Showing that other people are studying, quietly and without demand, is more motivating than any streak counter or notification.

The hardest decisions were about restraint: what to leave off the screen, when to celebrate, and how to make losing a streak feel meaningful without being punishing. The design system had to feel calm enough to sit next to your notes, but alive enough to make you want to check back in.

If I built this again, I'd spend more time on the map's ambient states: a heat map of study hotspots, quiet hours, or friends about to finish. There's a whole layer of social choreography still to design.
