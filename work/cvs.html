The Premise

FSA money has a deadline, and what it can buy is decided item by item

A health FSA is pre-tax money for medical costs, and most of it must be spent by the end of the plan year. In 2022, 52% of accountholders forfeited part of theirs, $441 on average.

Key tensions

It expires

Unused money is lost unless the plan allows a carryover, capped at $680 for 2026.

Eligibility is item by item

Pain relievers are eligible without a prescription. A chocolate bar in the same basket is not.

Shoppers do not see the split

The register charges the FSA only for eligible items, often without saying which ones.

What if the split the register already works out were visible before anyone pays?

Sources: EBRI forfeiture analysis, reported May 2024; IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section .15; Inventory Information Approval System; OPM on the CARES Act changes.

The Opportunity

The register already knows the answer

Chain pharmacies must run IIAS, a system that tags every product eligible or not and charges the FSA only the eligible total. Shoppers do not see that answer until they pay.

I started by designing an eligibility checker, then realised the register already calculates the answer.

Connecting a benefits account

Login stays with the provider

You log in on the provider's own page. CVS receives balances and plan dates and never sees the password.

The balance, and the part that expires

The deadline sits with the balance

Below the balance, the app shows how much expires on December 31 and how much can carry over into 2027. The HSA tab notes that HSA money does not expire.

Checking a product before buying it

Ineligible items show why

Reading glasses come back eligible. Chocolate comes back not eligible, because food is not a medical expense. Seeing the reason helps shoppers judge the next item themselves.

The split, while you shop

The cart shows the split

Each time the cart changes, the bar showing the FSA and card amounts updates.

From the phone to the register

At checkout, the register scans the barcode in the app and charges the eligible items to the FSA card. The app's balance then updates.

Itemized with eligibility

The Register

Four ways checkout can end

Not every shopper uses the app. The register's customer screen shows the same split to everyone and takes one card at a time.

Future Opportunities

What the design does not cover yet

A provider login that fails

Every login in the prototype succeeds. It needs states for a wrong password, a locked account and a provider that is down.

The app and the register disagree

The app's verdict and the register's list come from different places. When they differ, the screen needs to say which one applies.

Reflections

The register was harder to design than the app. Shoppers read their phone at their own pace, but at the register there is a line behind them, so each screen had to be quick to read.
