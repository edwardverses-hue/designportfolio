The Premise

FSA money expires, and only some items qualify

A health FSA is pre-tax money for medical costs.

It expires

Unspent money is lost, except a carryover of up to $680 (2026 limit).

Eligibility is item by item

Pain relievers count. A chocolate bar in the same basket does not.

The split comes at checkout

Shoppers see the eligible total only when they pay.

What if shoppers could see the FSA split before they reach the register?

Sources: IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section .15; OPM on the CARES Act changes; Inventory Information Approval System.

Research

People use their FSA, and still lose money

EBRI's analysis of 3.2 million flexible spending accounts shows how people actually use them. Most accountholders spend from their FSA during the year, and half still forfeit part of it.

Data insights

Using the account is not the gap

85% of accountholders spent from their FSA in 2022, and 52% still forfeited part of it.

Forfeiture is rising

The share who forfeited grew from 44% in 2019 to 52% in 2022. EBRI links the rise to the end of pandemic-era rollover rules.

Plan rules made no difference

EBRI found no significant difference in forfeiture between plans with a grace period or rollover and use-it-or-lose-it plans.

Eligibility rules changed in 2020

Since the CARES Act, over-the-counter medicines need no prescription. Vitamins and supplements are still not eligible.

Takeaway

Accountholders with a grace period or rollover forfeited at the same rate as those in strict plans, so plan rules are not where to intervene. The design puts the remaining balance, the deadline and the eligible amount in front of people at the moment they shop.

Sources: EBRI FSA analysis, reported May 2024; EBRI, Vital Statistics on FSAs, 2022; FSAFEDS on over-the-counter eligibility.

The Opportunity

The register already knows the answer

Chain pharmacies must run IIAS, a system that tags every product eligible or not and charges the FSA only the eligible total. The check runs when the card is presented, so the shopper first sees the eligible total at payment.

I started by designing an eligibility checker, then realised the register already calculates the answer.

Connecting a benefits account

Login stays with the provider

You log in on the provider's own page. CVS receives balances and plan dates and never sees the password.

The balance, and the part that expires

The deadline sits with the balance

Below the balance, the app shows how much expires on December 31 and how much can carry over into 2027. The HSA tab notes that HSA money does not expire.

Checking a product before buying it

Ineligible items show why

Reading glasses come back eligible. Chocolate comes back not eligible, because food is not a medical expense. The reasons follow IRS rules: over-the-counter medicines have counted without a prescription since 2020, while food and vitamins count only when a doctor prescribes them for a diagnosed condition.

The split, while you shop

The cart shows the split

Each time the cart changes, the bar showing the FSA and card amounts updates.

From the phone to the register

At checkout, the register scans the barcode in the app and charges the eligible items to the FSA card. The app's balance then updates.

The Register

Four ways checkout can end

Shoppers who do not use the app still pay at the register, so its customer screen shows the same split to everyone and takes one card at a time.

Future Opportunities

What the design does not cover yet

A provider login that fails

Every login in the prototype succeeds. It has no screens yet for a wrong password, a locked account or a provider outage.

The plan can refuse what the register approves

The register checks whether a product is eligible, and the shopper's plan can still decline it. A limited-purpose FSA covers only dental and vision, so the app needs a state for when the two disagree.

Sources: Forma on limited-purpose FSAs at pharmacies; American Benefits Group on IIAS approvals.

Reflections

The register was harder to design than the app. Shoppers read their phone at their own pace, but at the register there is a line behind them, so each screen had to be quick to read.
