Google Gemini Smart Context

A redesign of the Gemini home screen on iOS. I rebuilt the app screen by screen, then filled the empty space under its greeting with two stacks of suggestions: one for the time and place, one for recent work.

Problem

The home screen ignores what the app knows

Gemini opens on a greeting and a text field. The greeting uses your first name and nothing else, and nothing on the screen reflects what you did the last time you opened the app.

Most of the first screen is empty

The space between the greeting and the text field is the first thing people see, and holds nothing they can use. Nothing on it suggests the app can read a photograph or write working code.

Research

People skip the prompts and type their own question

I watched classmates open their AI assistants. Almost nobody read the suggested prompts: typing the question they already had was faster than reading the list. I then looked for published evidence; no assistant has published how often its prompts are clicked, so what follows is the nearest there is.

Generic suggestions go unclicked

In Nielsen Norman Group's tests of Amazon's shopping assistant, no participant clicked a suggested prompt on their own, including people who use Amazon daily. Suggestions drawn from the person do better: in a month-long test in a large chat assistant, starters built from each user's past sessions were clicked 9.4% more than the ones it showed before.

People go back for what they found before

In a year of search logs, as many as 40% of queries looked for something the person had already found, and in a follow-up study people misremembered their original query 30% of the time. Search is not chat, but going back to an old thread asks the same of memory.

Teevan et al., SIGIR 2007: a year of Yahoo logs from 114 people, and a study of 119 volunteers.

Going back to an old chat means remembering it

Once a chat leaves recents, search is the only way back, and you have to remember it exists and what it was called. The app knows the time, your location and your last conversation; none of it reaches the first screen.

The hour says less on a phone

What people ask moves with the clock: in Claude, requests for recipes are 2.3 times as frequent at 6 pm as on average. But across 37.5 million Copilot conversations, the top topic on phones, health, held at every hour, so the time alone is a weak signal on a phone.

Takeaway

Generic prompts get skipped, people go back to what they did before, and on a phone the hour alone says little. The home screen should lead with recent work, and use the time and place only when together they point at something specific.

Sources: Nielsen Norman Group on Amazon's Rufus, March 2025; Zheng et al., IceBreaker, April 2026, an industry preprint; Teevan et al., SIGIR 2007; Anthropic Economic Index, June 2026; Microsoft AI, Copilot Usage Report 2025. The search study is about the web, not chat; the 9.4% is a relative lift in an unnamed product.

Reframe

From better prompts to what the app already knows

My first idea was a row of suggested prompts under the greeting, but people skip those, and a better list was still a list. I changed the question to what the home screen should show, given what the app already knows about you.

From

"How might the home screen suggest better prompts?"

To

"How might the home screen use what Gemini already knows about you?"

An idea I set aside

Before this, I designed a fullscreen code view, because code blocks in the chat cut off long lines. Turned sideways, whole lines fit, but few people review code on a phone; they use a desk. I had found a real problem without checking how many people it affected, so I set it aside.

Components

Rebuilding Gemini before changing it

Gemini has no public design kit, so little came from a library. I drew Google Sans Flex, every icon, the status bar and two keyboards as vectors, matching each icon's corner rounding and stroke weight, then rebuilt each screen of a chat session.

From that recreation I documented its type, colour and components, sampling values from my renders, then added the stacks to it.

Typography

Colour

Google Sans Flex for the app, and SF Pro for anything iOS draws. Each size is recorded where it appears.

Blue from the glow behind the text field, blacks from the surfaces, greys from secondary text.

Components

Every part of a chat session is a component in one Figma file: bubbles, both keyboards, the drawer, the feedback sheets, the suggestion cards. The stacks are built from these, so they match the app.

Signing in, and getting around

Signing in is one button, since the Google account is already on the phone. The drawer lists new chat, search, images and library, then recents.

Rating an answer

Thumbs down opens a sheet with six reasons and a comment field. After you submit, the chat shows your rating and a one-line thank you.

Design

Suggestions from the time, the place and your last chat

The greeting stays, and two labelled stacks fill the space under it: the left for the time and place, the right for recent work. Each card names something specific, such as Shibam Coffee on Centre Avenue, open until 11 pm, or the next topic in the Python chat.

The stacks take their space from the text field, so each card has to be more useful than the question you were about to type.

Each stack's two cards

Glass cards

The cards are glass so the blue gradient behind Gemini's text field shows through them. A solid fill would be easier to read, but it would look added on top of the app.

The stacks move independently

The evening stack changes from coffee to dinner, then the work stack changes from the Python chat to the study app project.

Reflections

Check how many people a problem affects before designing for it

I would rebuild the app first again: until the screens were full size, I could not tell which empty areas were intentional. Two parts of the design are still open.

When a guess is wrong

The design does not yet say what happens when a suggestion misreads the time, the place or the task. In a study of proactive coding assistants, a third of suggestions went ignored or unnoticed.

What it knows about you

The stacks draw on your location and recent chats. The design does not yet show where you see that, or turn it off.

Sources: Pu et al., CHI 2025, 18 participants.
