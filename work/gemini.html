Google Gemini Smart Context

A redesign of Gemini's iOS home screen: two stacks of suggestions in the empty space under the greeting, one for the time and place, one for recent work.

Problem

The home screen ignores what the app knows

Gemini opens on a greeting and a text field. Nothing on it reflects what you did the last time you opened the app.

Most of the first screen is empty

Between the model picker and the text field there is only the greeting. Nothing on the screen hints that Gemini can read a photo or write code.

Research

People skip generic prompts, but use ones drawn from their own work

I watched classmates open their AI assistants. Almost nobody read the suggested prompts; they typed the question they already had. No assistant publishes how often its suggestions get tapped, so I looked for the closest published studies.

Generic suggestions go unclicked

In Nielsen Norman Group's 2025 tests of Amazon's shopping assistant, no participant clicked a suggested prompt without being told to. In a 2026 test in a large chat app, starters written from each user's past chats got 9.4% more clicks than generic ones.

Suggestions land between tasks, not during them

In a five-day field study, developers engaged with 52% of an assistant's suggestions when they came right after a commit. Mid-task, after an edit was declined, they dismissed 62%. Opening an app is a moment between tasks.

Kuo et al., IUI 2026: 15 developers, 229 suggestions in a code editor.

Gemini remembers your chats, but its home screen doesn't

Since August 2025, Gemini learns from past conversations by default, and Google describes the goal as a "personal, proactive" assistant. Yet to reopen a chat, you still scroll the side menu or search for it, and have to remember what it was called.

People do come back to chats: in 135,000 conversations donated by 300 ChatGPT users, a chat's first and last messages were 16.6 hours apart on average.

Time and place matter only some of the time

Some requests follow the clock: recipe requests to Claude are 2.3 times as frequent at 6 pm as on average. But across 37.5 million Copilot conversations, health was the top topic on phones at every hour. In a week-long field study of context-aware suggestions on phones, time and location mattered only in certain situations.

Takeaway

Suggestions get used when they come from a person's own chats and show up between tasks, as when the app opens. The time and place should add a card only when together they point to something specific, like dinner near campus at 6 pm.

Sources: Nielsen Norman Group on Amazon's Rufus, March 2025; Zheng et al., IceBreaker, ACL 2026 Industry Track; Kuo et al., Developer Interaction Patterns with Proactive AI, IUI 2026; 9to5Google on Gemini's personal context, August 2025; Karnam et al., Bowling with ChatGPT, ACM Web Conference 2026; Anthropic Economic Index, June 2026; Microsoft AI, Copilot Usage Report 2025; Chen et al., context-aware text entry on smartphones, CHI 2025. The 9.4% is a relative lift over generic starters, in a product the paper does not name; the 16.6 hours is an average pulled up by a few very long chats.

Reframe

From better prompts to what the app already knows

My first idea was a row of better prompts, but people skip prompts. So I asked what the screen should show, given what Gemini already knows.

From

"How might the home screen suggest better prompts?"

To

"How might the home screen use what Gemini already knows about you?"

Solution

Two stacks of suggestions under the greeting

The home screen's empty space holds suggestions from what Gemini already knows about you.

Two stacks One tap Time, location, recent chats Under the greeting Sends the card's question for you
Components

Rebuilding Gemini before changing it

Gemini has no public design kit, so I rebuilt its screens from my own screenshots, drawing the icons, the status bar and both keyboards as vectors.

Then I documented its type, colour and components, and built the stacks from them.

Typography

Colour

Google Sans Flex for the app's own text, and SF Pro for what iOS draws, like the status bar and keyboard.

Blues from the glow behind the text field, blacks from the backgrounds, greys from secondary text.

Components

Every part of a chat is a component in one Figma file, so the stacks are built from the same parts as the app around them.

Design Breakdown

Suggestions from the time, the place and your last chat

They sit just above the text field, so a card is only worth showing if it is more useful than the question you were about to type.

The first two cards in each stack

Glass cards

The cards are frosted glass, so the blue glow behind the text field shows through them. Solid cards would be easier to read but would look added on top of the app.

Each stack changes on its own

Each stack holds several cards, with dots underneath to show how many. They change one at a time, so the screen never shifts all at once: here coffee turns to dinner, then the Python chat to the study app project.

A card from recent work picks up the chat

Tapping it reopens the Python chat and sends the next step the card suggested, so Gemini answers where you left off.

A card for the evening asks the question for you

Tapping it starts a new chat with the question already sent, and Gemini answers with places near campus that are open late.

Reflections

Rebuild an app before changing it

Rebuilding Gemini first was worth it: only at full size could I tell which empty space was deliberate. Two parts are still open.

When a guess is wrong

Nothing yet says what happens when a suggestion misreads the time, place or task. In a study of coding assistants, a third of proactive suggestions went ignored.

What it knows about you

The stacks use your location and recent chats. Nothing yet shows you that, or lets you turn it off.

Sources: Pu et al., CHI 2025, 18 participants.
